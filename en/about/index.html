The Two Core Purposes of the Project

Why we do this

Two-Way Talent Cultivation

“Two-way” here means that professional knowledge flows to the community, and abilities flow back to our students. Students of a medical university translate their knowledge into language the community can easily understand and bring it into the field; through this service, they learn the communication skills and empathy that every health professional needs. Children in rural communities, through science classes and arts activities, step beyond familiar ways of thinking and discover their own possibilities.

Sustainable Development of Healthy Communities

This project will not be here forever. So our aim is not simply to bring health services in, but to build a correct understanding of health in the community, make it a shared understanding among residents, and help everyone reinforce one another in positive ways. Drawing on the professional practicum courses of our departments and the resources of our affiliated hospitals, we work over the long term in three areas: developmental assessments for young children, oral care for all ages, and community fitness. When brushing, exercise, and regular check-ups become part of daily life, health can remain even after the project leaves.

The Three Pillars of the Project

What we do

Pillar 1

Compassionate Companionship and Diverse Exploration

The STEAM science classes of the STEAM Science Education Program spark children’s motivation to learn and their spirit of inquiry, supported by music activities and after-school tutoring. The goal is not remedial teaching, but to widen children’s imagination of the world and help them discover their own possibilities.

  • STEAM Science Education Program — Saturday science exploration classes, the core activity of the project
  • Baolai Music Camp and joint concerts — the KMU Vocal Music Club with local choirs
  • Tutoring in rural communities — our students support learners at the Nibun Learning Center
  • University major talks at senior high schools — KMU professors visit schools to introduce their fields
  • City and university visits — including the KMU OPEN HOUSE, with activities from every department
  • Elementary school students visit KMU — a hands-on caregiver experience class

Pillar 2

School and Community Health Promotion

Drawing on the practicum courses of our departments and the resources of our affiliated hospitals, we work from developmental assessments for young children to oral care for all ages and fitness promotion. The goal is to make health care a shared understanding in the community, not just a one-time service.

Health education for people with special needs

Syin-Lu Foundation

A concrete example

Most members of the Syin-Lu Foundation day service center in Liugui have intellectual disabilities and need repeated, targeted guidance. Every month, faculty and students of the Department of Oral Hygiene visit the center to practice with them; after one year, most members had learned to brush their teeth properly. Local dentists observe that severe tooth decay which once required root canal treatment can now usually be treated with a simple filling.

In addition, according to the records of the Syin-Lu Foundation’s Liugui center, members’ midday tooth-brushing rate has remained at 100%.

Pillar 3

Medical Humanities and Sustainable Service

From theory on campus and hands-on coursework to practice in the field, these form a complete path of growth. By designing lessons, students of our medical university practice translating their professional knowledge; by working with residents, they see the social factors behind health and confirm their professional identity.

  • Laonong field walk — first-year students visit the area and talk with local elders
  • STEAM Innovation Cultivation course — systematic training for STEAM Science Education Program instructors
  • Social Practice Micro-Program — service learning as part of the formal curriculum
  • Exhibitions — including the oral health education board game at the 2025 USR EXPO
  • Student-made teaching tools — an oral health board game developed by students, taken into the field, and handed over to a new cohort (see below)
  • Inter-university collaboration — MOUs signed with Chang Jung Christian University, Shih Chien University and National Pingtung University; in Spring 2026 (Academic Year 2025–26), the Crucible Club and the Fu Club held an online exchange with the College of Education of National Pingtung University

A Student-Made Health Education Tool

An oral health board game, from development to handover

From a student proposal, to the STEAM Science Education Program, and then handed over to a new cohort of students — a teaching tool that is also a path of talent cultivation.

Spring 2025 (Academic Year 2024–25)

Developed by a student team

Students from the School of Dentistry, the Department of Oral Hygiene, the School of Medicine, the School of Pharmacy, the Department of Medicinal and Applied Chemistry, and the Department of Sports Medicine formed a team. From the proposal and design workshops to the final rules, all content and artwork were created by students.

→

Fall 2025 (Academic Year 2025–26)

Printed and taken into the STEAM Science Education Program

The full set of 156 cards was printed and brought into the STEAM Science Education Program at Longxing Elementary School. The four key points of the Bass brushing technique became “cleaning cards,” and 17 kinds of event cards cover the causes of tooth decay, fluoride products, regular check-ups, and oral cancer prevention.

→

Fall 2026 (Academic Year 2026–27)

Workshops, handed over to a new cohort

We held board game development workshops for the Fu Club team, inviting professional instructors to teach game development and mechanics design. The first session took place on September 22, 2026, and a new cohort of students is taking over development, from mechanics design to playtesting and revision.

Under faculty supervision, students of the Department of Oral Hygiene also completed an on-campus undergraduate research project using this board game.

Turning a one-time teaching tool project into a talent cultivation model that can be passed on year after year.

A Continuing Path

From a rural community, and back again

One young woman started in the Nibun Choir, went on to KMU, became president of the Vocal Music Club, and then brought more than fifty students back to Baolai Junior High School to hold a music camp.

“Children in rural communities actually have few chances to dream.”
— President of the KMU Vocal Music Club (1111 News, February 2026)

This path from a rural community to the university and back again was paved by Nibun and the doctor who coaches its choir; our project continues along it.

Project History

Starting from Provincial Highway 27, a continuing journey

Project phases and the evolution of the pillars; compiled from the project proposals, the annual achievement reports, and the activity records on the Higher Education Sprout Project website.

  1. Since 2015

    KMU faculty and students come to Liugui and Taoyuan

    Local service began with health services and oral health education in communities along the Laonong River.

  2. 2020

    USR-HUB Seed Project

    Carried out in two areas: health care and talent cultivation.

  3. 2023

    Phase 3 (2023–2024)

    The seed project advanced to become a project of the Ministry of Education University Social Responsibility Program, and the name “Sustainable Laonong Talent Cultivation and Health Development Plan” has been used since then; the work was organized around four pillars. The STEAM Science Education Program began in Fall 2023 (Academic Year 2023–24).

  4. 2025

    Phase 4 (2025–2027) | Current

    The project name and field sites continue; the 2026 revised proposal merged the four pillars into the current three.

  5. From 2028

    Phase 5 | In planning

    Under Ministry of Education rules, Phase 5 will apply as a more advanced project type. International cooperation and cross-border talent cultivation are listed as future directions, and pilots in new field sites are being evaluated; the content is still in planning.
