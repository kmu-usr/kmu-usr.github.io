A young person who has walked this whole path

A KMU alumnus, a doctor, returned to Liugui and has worked there for more than ten years.
The Nibun Choir he coaches has won gold at the World Choir Games and has accompanied a generation of Liugui children as they grew up.
Our project continues this path: the KMU Vocal Music Club works with local school choirs over the long term, holding a music camp and joint concerts every year.

One young woman is a child of this very path. She grew up in the Nibun Choir, was admitted to Kaohsiung Medical University, and became president of the Vocal Music Club, a partner club of this project.

In the 2026 winter break, she led 55 KMU students back to Baolai Junior High School in Liugui to hold a music camp for 43 junior high school students, using her own school experience to tell them: children from rural communities can dream boldly, too.

“Children in rural communities
actually have few chances to dream.”
— President of the KMU Vocal Music Club (1111 News, February 2026)

Those who once received support grow up to serve, and return home to guide the next generation.
And at the start of this path, a third-grade child wrote on a STEAM Science Education Program feedback form:

“I’m very happy, because the big brothers and sisters are with me.”

The starting point and the destination are one and the same.

Why companies are needed

Not because we lack funds now, but because children’s growth should not rise and fall with budget cycles.

STEP 01

The services already run steadily

Every activity has run for years with Ministry of Education USR funding. This is not a project waiting for start-up money, but an established, proven mechanism that is already supporting children.

STEP 02

But government projects have cycles

USR projects are reviewed and approved phase by phase. We are building a long-term base of support for these services that does not depend on a single government budget.

STEP 03

Corporate support = continuity

It keeps the occupational therapy team visiting preschools, and keeps the wheels that carry university students to rural communities turning, even when a phase of funding ends.

What you support is not one event,
but continuity.

Outcomes for University Students | IRB-approved scale-based follow-up study (since Spring 2025, Academic Year 2024–25)

2025 pilot study: KMU students in the STEAM Science Education Program (38 students) showed statistically significant pre- and post-test differences in two dimensions, life satisfaction and academic growth (paired t-tests, corrected for multiple comparisons; effect sizes d = 0.60 and 0.70), and 76.3% said they were willing to continue serving.

2026 (Spring 2026, Academic Year 2025–26) interim outcome evaluation: matched non-participating students served as a comparison group (after excluding invalid responses: 30 in the participant group and 50 in the comparison group; the full sample is reported in the original report). Participating students’ average scores rose in all four areas: interpersonal relationships, life satisfaction, academic achievement and overall well-being, and 70% improved in overall well-being. Over the same period, the comparison group stayed level or declined on every dimension, with 54% declining. The improvement shares of the two groups differed by 34 percentage points, with a between-group effect size of d = 0.52 (medium); a Bayesian analysis estimated a 98.3% probability that the participant group improved more than the comparison group.

Sources: project pilot study (2025) and outcome evaluation report (2026). Both use a pre- and post-test scale design; the outcome evaluation uses a matched comparison group without random assignment. The results describe changes in participating students and do not support causal conclusions.
